# Torsion-Based Computation

## Overview

Yupana CRT introduces the possibility that periodic modular dynamics may act as computational resources rather than merely mathematical artifacts.

This document outlines the conceptual foundations of torsion-oriented computation.

---

# 1. Dynamical Background

Given:

\[
f_k(x)=x^k \bmod n
\]

the modular phase space decomposes into:

- attractors
- transient structures
- periodic cycles
- torsional regions

under repeated evolution.

---

# 2. Torsion Definition

For each CRT-local component:

\[
\mathbb{Z}_{p^\alpha}
\]

the periodic structure of an orbit defines a local torsion value:

\[
\tau_{p^\alpha}(x)
\]

representing orbital periodicity.

The complete torsional signature becomes:

\[
\vec{\tau}(x)
\]

across all modular components.

---

# 3. Torsion as Information

The phase position inside a periodic orbit may encode state information.

This differs from classical binary encoding.

Information becomes associated with:

- orbital phase
- periodic structure
- attractor geometry
- dynamical position

rather than static symbolic values alone.

---

# 4. Torsion-Aware Execution

A torsion-sensitive execution system may support:

- oscillatory computation
- periodic synchronization
- phase-aligned routing
- cycle-based memory
- modular recurrent dynamics

inside finite algebraic spaces.

---

# 5. CRT Synchronization

Distinct CRT components may exhibit different periodic structures.

Their interaction through CRT reconstruction creates composite dynamical behavior governed by:

\[
\operatorname{lcm}(L_1,L_2,\dots,L_r)
\]

where each:

\[
L_i
\]

is a local cycle length.

---

# 6. Computational Interpretation

Under this framework:

- cycles become computational states
- phase becomes structure
- periodicity becomes memory
- torsion becomes a dynamical degree of freedom

inside modular execution systems.

---

# 7. Difference from Classical Computation

Classical architectures primarily manipulate:

- static symbolic states
- sequential arithmetic transformations

Torsion-oriented systems instead emphasize:

- dynamical phase evolution
- periodic modular geometry
- orbit-aware processing

---

# 8. Difference from Quantum Computation

Torsion-based computation is:

- discrete
- deterministic
- algebraic
- finite-state

It does not rely on:

- quantum amplitudes
- superposition
- probabilistic collapse
- continuous Hilbert spaces

although some structural analogies may exist at the level of periodic behavior.

---

# 9. Open Questions

Major unresolved questions include:

- computational expressiveness
- complexity behavior
- scalability
- hardware efficiency
- learning capability
- synchronization stability

---

# 10. Research Status

Torsion-based computation remains an exploratory theoretical direction.

No claim is made regarding:

- computational superiority
- universality
- industrial applicability
- practical deployment

until rigorous validation is achieved.
